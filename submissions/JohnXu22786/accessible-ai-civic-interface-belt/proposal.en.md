It is not a statutory plan, engineering conclusion, investment commitment, partnership, approval, or government finding. ## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

Overall design treats accessibility as a city interface: people can find a person, understand uncertainty, return to paper or human help after failure, and see how their feedback was handled. The spatial structure is west service intake - central co-translation and testing - east evening life. North-south continuity is studied through existing public-space, community-entry, and walking relationships; no unverified road, rail, or bridge engineering conclusion is drawn. [source:SRC-MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN] [source:SRC-BARRIER-FREE-LAW]

Renewal follows retain interface, improve access, remove little, and keep fallback. Retain legible railway-memory cues, mature green, usable buildings, and community services. Improve entrances, ground-floor interfaces, walls, signs, lighting, and accessible routes. Demolition is only a future investigation list for illegal, unsafe, or genuinely blocking objects; this concept does not name parcels, areas, or owners. FAR, height, underground space, investment, and construction cost are not decided here. [source:SRC-MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING]

Design rules: continuous interface; layered information in plain Chinese, English, large type, audio, and paper; reversible components first; and blue-green verification before water-edge activity. These are design principles, not regulatory conclusions.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green strategy does not turn water-edge experience into an engineering promise. It builds an observable, reversible, verification-first public-space sequence: the Jingzhang Heritage Park is a cultural narrative context; node interfaces use light-touch seats, shade, signs, exhibits, and low-bandwidth services; the Xiaoyuehe wing is a public testing and environmental-observation interface. Shoreline, blue-line, river-management, flood, and planting matters await official material and professional review. [source:SRC-TASKBOOK-CLEARED]

Three conceptual AI landmarks are identifiers, not buildings or trademarks: the Doorframe Timeline for railway history and current AI question cards; the Co-translation Beacon for non-tracking text/audio cues about human service and public issues; and the Access Scale for tactile, icon, and bilingual cues for route, pause, and fallback. Each is removable, stoppable, and replaceable. No unlicensed historic images, fonts, portraits, or trademarks are used.

The component library includes bilingual gate signs, large-type/tactile signs, low information desks, folding seats, reversible shade, quiet cues, paper-map boxes, human-fallback boards, data-status boards, comment boxes, and activity-accessibility check cards. Color and contrast are design suggestions requiring color-vision, night, screen-reader, and field-accessibility testing.

Agent.4 defines the “Jingzhang Heritage Park AI public space” as a light-touch concept: an east-west stitch connects the cultural entry and industry-service interface, while a north-south continuity connects community entries, pocket spaces, and the public-experience route. Dazhongsi is treated as a reversible test of intelligent-native consumer and business service interfaces, not an unauthorized alteration of titled property. The honor-display system is a removable public ledger of source cards, voluntary contributor credit, failure/repair records, and annual disclosure; it avoids portraits, corporate marks, and unlicensed material. The public-space component library turns gate signs, tactile signs, service desks, status boards, and human-fallback boards into replaceable modules.

Agent.5’s spatial culture system has three linked narratives: the railway timeline, Zhongguancun innovation practice, and AI-native culture question cards. Its carriers include wayfinding, signs, symbols, paper/audio source cards, node exhibits, and bilingual web copy. The international communication narrative presents a city character in which history is understandable, technology can be questioned, and service can be exited.
